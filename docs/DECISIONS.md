# Underkeep — Decisions and Open Questions

Rulings here override the other documents. Add new entries at the top of each list, with a date.

## Decisions

- **2026-09-19 — Item effects through hooks, and the fight that finally reads them.** `src/engine/item-hooks.js` is the other half of an item's effects: the ones that need a hit, a kill or a round to have happened. `src/engine/item-actions.js` is the Item action of `06` section 4, and `src/systems/use-item.js` is the pack side of it. Eight rulings:
  - **A hero's own hooks were never registered.** `registerSkills` existed from Phase 4 and nothing called it, so Cleave, Regeneration and Mana Siphon did nothing in a real fight. `registerRules` now registers the hero's skills *and* their gear, which is where a fight's rules belong, and the skills tests build their fight the same way.
  - **A die of damage is a hook; a flat point is a sheet number.** Flaming's 1d6 fire has to know about the hit — the type it deals, who it is dealt to, whether the blow crit — so it hangs on `damageCalc`. Mighty's +2 does not, so it stays on the sheet. `items.json` was changed to say so: seven effects moved from `sheet` to `hook`.
  - **The bestiary's `type` column is a family, and now reaches the unit.** Holy weapons, Holy Water and the mace's "+1 vs undead" all ask what a monster *is*, and `makeMonster` was dropping it (`type` on the unit is the monster's id). Units now carry `family`, and Turn Undead and Smite will read the same field.
  - **A potion's buff is a layer on the sheet, not a condition.** `01` section 7's conditions are a closed list and a potion is not on it, so a buff is `{ effects, until }` on `hero.buffs`, folded by the same code that folds the skills and the gear, dropped at the end of the fight or counted down in steps. Quicksilver is the exception that was already anticipated: `bandOf` has read `unit.actsFirst` since Phase 3.
  - **A scroll is the skill it casts, for no Focus.** `actionForItem` builds the skill's own action at the reader's level and modifiers and sets `fp: 0` (`04` section 9). A scroll whose skill is not built yet says so by name, the way a dimmed skill button does.
  - **A use whose system is not built yet says "not here".** Mapping, Teleport, Return, camping, Dust of Revealing and the lock-openers are listed in `NOT_YET` with what each waits for, so the button explains itself instead of failing.
  - **An armour property waives what the armour costs, rather than paying the hero back.** Featherlight, Shadowed and Spellwoven change how the *item* is read, so `gearSummary` answers them where it reads the item and they never reach the sheet.
  - **A granted skill is a skill.** `skillsOf(hero)` folds what the gear lends into what the hero learned, deduped and at the better rank, and both the sheet and the hook registration read that list — so Silkweave's Evasion adds its +1 DEF and Warden's Mace's Turn Undead will register when Turn Undead exists.

- **2026-09-19 — Cursed items, and the effects that finally land.** `src/systems/gear.js` is `04` section 6: a cursed item binds itself the moment it is worn, does what its curse says, and comes off only two ways. Making the eight curses *do* something needed the general path for an item's `sheet` and `explore` effects, so the sheet-shaped half of every item effect — charms, magic robes, weapon and armour properties — works from here too. Seven rulings:
  - **Binding is the pack's, revealing is identification's, and the sheet is the sheet's.** `equip` sets `bound` on a cursed item and `unequip` already refused a bound one; `wear(hero, id)` in `gear.js` is the one call that does all three in order, so no screen has to remember the sequence.
  - **An item's `sheet` effects fold in with the skills'.** `gearSummary` gathers what the worn items, their properties and their curses declare, and the gear step of `applySkillSheet` folds every `sheet` one through the same `applySheetEffect` a skill uses. Five new entries in that vocabulary carry the curses: `initiative`, `damage`, `attributeMod`, `cannot`, and `explore` flags. The `hook` half — Flaming's fire die, Vampiric, Thorned — is the next task.
  - **A flat damage bonus is a sheet number, not a hook.** Bloodthirsty's +2 and the Mighty property's +2 are `{ sheet: "damage", value: 2 }`, so `damage.js` reads `attacker.damageBonus` beside the attribute modifier, the way it already reads `dr`. An effect that needs to know about the hit — a die of fire, a type, a kind of attack — is left for a hook.
  - **Unlucky narrows the critical range.** `01` section 4 hangs the 19-20 range on "the LCK mod is +2 or better", and the curse says "-2 to your LCK mod", so the range is recomputed from where Luck ended up, keeping whatever Weapon Mastery widened. A cursed hero who loses the wide range gets it back when the curse goes.
  - **Bloodthirsty's ban is a list on the sheet.** `{ sheet: "cannot", actions: ["defend", "flee"] }` becomes `hero.cannot`, and `legalityOf` dims any action whose tags match with the reason `cursedGrip`. The engine never learns what a curse is.
  - **Unidentified gear still works.** A charm's +1 DEF applies from the moment it is worn even though the hero has no idea why — `04` section 5 reveals a charm "when it first triggers", which it could not do otherwise.
  - **A freed item is not a named one.** A Scroll of Remove Curse lifts the curse and the binding, and leaves a sword of unknown bonus, because knowing what the bonus is still takes a combat (`04` section 5). The negative bonus stays: the curse is gone, the bad steel is not.

- **2026-09-19 — Per-game looks and titles, and the eight ways to learn what something is.** `src/systems/identification.js` is `04` section 5: the shuffle that makes a Murky Potion a different thing in every game, and the table of methods over it. Seven rulings:
  - **The shuffle has a stream of its own.** `appearanceStream(masterSeed)` joins layout, restock and creation as a derived stream (`05` section 11's shape, never saved). The save stores the result as `04` section 17 asks, and it can always be rebuilt from the seed — so a save that loses it is not a save that loses the game.
  - **Eighteen looks, and titles made from pairs.** Seventeen potions are found unknown and no two may share a look. Scroll titles are every ordered pair of the sixteen words, shuffled and dealt, so all nineteen scrolls differ and *"VORN ESKEL"* is one of the pairs that can come up.
  - **Knowledge is per type for potions and scrolls, per item for gear.** `04` says one identified potion identifies every potion of the type, so `knownTypes` is the authority for those, and an instance's own flags are the authority for gear.
  - **Gear is learned one secret at a time.** "The bonus shows after one combat. A property shows when it first triggers. A curse reveals itself immediately" is three separate reveals, so an item carries `revealed: ['bonus']` and is only `identified` once it has no secrets left. That is what lets the game show *"Long Sword +2"* before it shows *"Flaming Long Sword +2"*.
  - **A charm's secret is what it does.** A charm has no bonus to reveal, so `secretsOf` gives it one secret, `effect`, which a hundred steps or its first trigger lets out. Magic robes are the same.
  - **Every charm carries a plain word to hide behind.** `04` writes an unknown charm as *"Plain Ring"*, which needs a word that is not its name: `plain` is now a field on all thirty charms, and a data check fails a new charm without one. Two rings read alike, which is the point.
  - **An unknown item never shows its rarity.** `describe` returns `rarity: "unknown"` until the item is known, because a red border on an unidentified item would reveal the curse that `04` says is revealed by *wearing* it.

- **2026-09-19 — Loot generation.** `src/systems/loot.js` is `04` section 14 over `02` section 17: one d100 + (LCK mod x 5) for the encounter, a d12 on the category's table, and then the Gear Details — the base type, the bonus the floor allows, the property, the curse check, and the identification state everything arrives in. Every draw is on the **loot** stream, so a fight on a seed always drops the same things. Seven rulings:
  - **A drop is not an item yet.** It comes back as `{ baseId, count, rarity, identified, bonus?, property?, curse?, foundOnFloor }` — exactly what `inventory.addItem` takes — and the pack is what gives it an instance id. That keeps ids in one place and lets the Victory screen show a drop before anyone picks it up.
  - **Order is part of the roll.** `lootAfterCombat` takes each defeated monster's own drops first and then the encounter's table roll. Either order matches the documents; the stream is saved, so the order had to be fixed and written down.
  - **A charm is magic even with no bonus.** `04` section 6 curses "weapons, armor, shields, and charms", and a charm has no bonus to turn negative, so a cursed charm keeps its curse and nothing else. Magic robes are the same.
  - **A cursed item's property is rolled fresh.** "Half of cursed items also carry a normal property" reads as a roll of its own, not as keeping whatever the item would have had — so an Uncommon +1 weapon that turns out cursed can carry a property, which is what makes one tempting.
  - **Seeking rerolls until it is not Seeking.** `04` section 4 says "(Melee weapons reroll)" without saying how often; rerolling once could still land on Seeking. The loop is bounded by the size of the die.
  - **`rarity: "cursed"` is on the instance from the moment it drops, and `identified: false` with it.** `04` section 1 says the red only shows "once revealed", so the rarity is what the item *is* and the identification state is what the screen may show. A screen that colours an unidentified item by its rarity would give the curse away.
  - **Legendaries are removed from the pool by the caller's set.** `rollLoot` takes a `found` collection (a Set or the save's array) and adds to it. Nothing persists it yet: the run state that carries it between floors is Phase 6's, and until then a fresh set means a legendary could be found twice across two runs of the same seed.

- **2026-09-19 — The pack, and the stand-in it replaced.** `src/systems/inventory.js` is `04` section 1: ten slots plus twice the Might modifier, stacks of five and ten, the four equipment slots, the four quick slots. `src/systems/kit.js` now hands over `04`'s own items, and `src/data/starting-kit.json` is **gone** — the Phase 4 note said it would go the moment `items.json` arrived, and it has. Eight rulings:
  - **A pack is plain data on the hero.** `{ capacity, items, equipped, quick, nextId }`, with `equipped` holding instance ids rather than copies: an equipped item is still in `items`, and the slot count skips it (`04` section 1: equipped gear costs no slots). One id means one thing wherever it turns up, which is what the save, the Pack screen and the quick bar all need.
  - **Stacking is by identity, not by name.** Two items stack only if they are the same base, both plain, and both known — a +1 dagger never joins a pile of daggers, and an unknown potion never joins a known one. Anything that does not stack gets an entry of its own, so three daggers are three daggers and any one of them can be thrown.
  - **`refreshGear` is the only reader of the pack.** It writes `hero.weapon`, `hero.armor`, `hero.attack` and `hero.gear` — a summary of DEF, Max AGI, to-hit, fizzle and stealth. The engine reads the summary and never learns what an item is, the same way it reads a monster's unit rather than its stat block.
  - **Gear folds into the sheet after the skills.** Armor Training cancels heavy armour's to-hit and fizzle (`04` section 3), so the gear cannot go into the base: `applySkillSheet` composes base -> skills -> gear. DEF is the exception and stays in the base, because `01` section 4's formula names armour in it.
  - **The two `while` bonuses now hold.** Duelist's +1 DEF with no shield and Armor Training's rank 2 DR in heavy armour were written off as "waiting for the pack". The pack answers both, and `test/skills.test.js` checks the Duelist's +1 appears and goes again when a shield goes on.
  - **Armour's -2 to hit is on melee and ranged, not on spells.** `04` section 3 charges heavy armour twice over — to-hit *and* a spell fizzle chance — so reading the to-hit as covering spell attacks would tax them twice. The fizzle is on the sheet as `hero.spellFizzle`, waiting for a spell to fail.
  - **A requirement not met is a penalty, not a refusal.** `04` says the hero may wear armour they cannot really carry and takes -2 to hit while the DEF still counts, so `equip` allows it and `gearSummary` reports it in `unmet` for the screen to mark.
  - **The kit goes straight into its slots.** `equipNew` puts the origin's weapon and armour in their slots without passing through the pack, so a hero with few slots still starts dressed; what will not fit afterwards is recorded on `hero.kitLeftBehind` rather than silently lost.

- **2026-09-19 — `items.json` and `loot.json`.** The first of Phase 5: `04` sections 1 to 13 as one data file, section 14's three d12 tables as another, with `items.js` and `loot.js` reading them. **155 base items**, which is every row of every table in `04` — 17 weapons, 9 armours and shields, 3 magic robes, 26 charms, 19 potions, 19 scrolls, 8 bombs, 15 pieces of gear, 11 valuables, 11 monster parts, 10 boss rewards and 6 legendaries. Nothing generates loot yet; these are the tables it will read. Eight rulings:
  - **The document's own JSON shape wins.** `04` section 17 prints the template, so an item is `{ id, name, category, group, damage, damageType, properties, requires, slots, cost }` — `category`, not `type`, and the name in the item, as `monsters.json` keeps a monster's. The tables under the items (bonuses, properties, curses, gems, identification, the Alchemist's recipes) live in the same file, because they only ever describe items.
  - **Two kinds of number, and one vocabulary for effects.** An item's own keys describe the thing — `damage`, `toHit`, `def`, `maxAgi`, `slots`. Its `effects` are what it adds to the *hero* while it is held, written in `skills.json`'s vocabulary (`sheet`, `hook`, `explore`, `action`), so a charm reaches the sheet the way a skill does and no item gets a code path of its own. Two kinds are new: `shop` (the Merchant's Seal) and `grants` (a unique that lends a skill, `04` section 13). Handlers for the item hooks come with the next task; the data check validates the event names now.
  - **Scroll ids are `scroll_of_*`.** `origins.json` already carried `scroll_of_sleep` from Phase 4, and the Apprentice's kit has to keep working.
  - **Eighteen potion appearances, not twelve.** Section 5 names twelve; seventeen potion types are found unknown, and two sharing a look would be a bug the player could not see past. The twelve named come first, then Cloudy, Golden, Inky, Ashen, Swirling and Pale. Scroll titles are two of sixteen nonsense words (*"VORN ESKEL"* is the document's example, and both words are in the list).
  - **"Unique to its floor" is not a rarity.** The Rune Key and the Floor Key are `rarity: "common"` with `floorBound: true`; `unique` is kept for the gold once-per-game items of section 13, which is what the rarity table means by it.
  - **A loot pool is a filter, not a list.** `loot.json`'s `pools` name a category and a rarity, and the pool is every matching item — so an item joins its own table by existing. Two flags keep things out: `loot: false` for the found-only and crafted items (the keys, Dragonscale Mail), and `harmful: true` for section 8's four bad potions, which are never a reward.
  - **The Ratcatcher's Charm is immune to a tag, not a condition.** There is no `diseased` condition in `01` section 7 — the Giant Rat's Filthy Bite applies `weakened` — so the charm carries `{ sheet: "immune", tags: ["disease"] }`, and the tag is what a disease effect will be marked with.
  - **The category roll lives in `loot.json`.** `02` section 17's d100 + (LCK mod x 5) decides Common, Uncommon or Rare, and `04` section 14 decides the item. They are one roll followed by another, so they are one file.

- **2026-09-19 — The climb to level 5, and the four things it found missing.** `npm run climb` is the Phase 4 "done when": it rolls a hero through character creation, walks floors 1 and 2 through the same run the Exploration screen drives, fights what the step clock turns up through the same `createFight` the Combat screen drives, and levels them on the XP that pays. **It holds: 24 of 24 Adventurers reach level 5**, in a median of 33 trips and 46 fights. Six rulings:
  - **A hero carries their kit from the first step.** `01` section 3 hands out a long sword and leather; `04` says what those are worth. `starting-kit.json` holds the two numbers a fight reads — a weapon's dice and an armour's DEF — and goes the moment `items.json` arrives (a data check enforces that every equipped kit item has a line). Without it a created hero was *worse* than Phase 3's stand-in, who had a weapon and armour baked in.
  - **Weapon damage adds the attacker's own modifier.** `01` section 8 and `06` section 7 step 4: MIG for melee, AGI for a thrown weapon, nothing for a bow or a spell. Nothing was reading it. Only a unit with attributes adds one, because a monster's damage line already carries its flat.
  - **Active skills are playable.** `skills.json`'s `action` blocks were data nothing used: the Combat: Skills sheet listed a hero's skills and every one of them was dimmed. `skill-actions.js` turns one into the action the turn engine already resolves — an attacking skill goes through every step of sections 6 and 7 as a swing does, and a heal is resolved there. A shape no phase has built yet (whole-row spells, wards, cures, the exploration skills) is offered and refused **by name**, so the sheet says what it is waiting for.
  - **A fight leaves nothing on the hero.** Now that the hero unit is the hero, one fight's flight, telegraph, initiative and lost turns would be there at the start of the next. `COMBAT_LEFTOVERS` in `field.js` is the list that is cleared, and hit points, Focus and conditions are pointedly not on it.
  - **The climb is a careful player, not a clever one.** It heals below 60%, runs below 50%, turns back to town below 80%, and otherwise hits what is in front of it. No positioning, no target choice, no saving Focus. If *that* reaches level 5, a player can.
  - **Death is what the mode says it is.** `05` section 9: an Adventurer wakes in town having lost half their gold and goes down again, keeping their level; an Ironman's climb is over. The tool models both, and only the Adventurer number is the phase's promise.

- **2026-09-19 — Victory and Loot, Level Up, and the three wires under them.** `src/ui/screens/loot.js` and `src/ui/screens/levelup.js` are the outline's "Combat and Rewards" tables. Neither screen decides anything: `06` section 15 pays the XP, the gold and the levels as combat ends, and `05` section 11 wants them committed before they are shown. Six rulings:
  - **The hero unit *is* the hero.** `toUnit` copies a monster template — two rats are two objects — but the hero is the one person the run keeps, so the hero unit is the hero's own object. Everything a fight does then happens to whoever walked in.
  - **The gold is rolled as combat ends, on the `loot` stream.** `06` section 15 step 4 pays the defeated monsters' own gold on top of what a fleeing monster dropped; `endCombat` now takes the stream and rolls it, and the fight pays it into the purse before the screen draws it. A reload cannot re-roll the purse.
  - **The Victory screen reports, it does not award.** XP, gold and levels are already banked when it opens; the LEVEL UP chip is on when the fight earned one, and CONTINUE goes to Level Up when it did and back to the dungeon when it did not.
  - **TAKE ALL says why it has nothing.** Drops are Phase 5, so a victory pays coin and the drop list carries one line saying so. The button and the per-drop TAKE are built; only the items are missing.
  - **The attribute point is picked on the screen and spent on CONFIRM.** Everything else about a level already happened. A fight worth several levels shows one card each, and CONFIRM walks to the next — the last one leaves for the dungeon.
  - **A level carries the totals it reached.** `levelUp` returns `maxHp` and `maxFp` as well as the difference, because the screen draws "22 → 25".

  **The two bugs this found.** The router copied its context with a spread, so a host's `run` and `fight` getters were read once, when the router was made: a screen drew the run as it was at start-up — the Victory screen showed a fight that had not been fought, and a hero made in creation would never have reached the screens at all. The context is now copied by its property descriptors, so the getters stay live. And with the hero unit a copy, a fight paid its XP, its levels and its gold to an object the run threw away.

- **2026-09-19 — The Hero screens, and the 21st skill point.** `src/ui/screens/hero.js` and `src/ui/screens/skill-tree.js` are the outline's "Hero and Items" tables, with `src/ui/parts/hero-header.js` holding the rows the three Hero screens share. Five rulings:
  - **The header is declared once.** `heroHeaderRegions()` returns rows 1–2 and 3–4 for whichever screen asks, so Stats, the Skill Tree and — when `04` arrives — the Pack cannot drift apart. When the frame is wide the tabs sit *beside* the bar rather than under it: nine rows is not enough to spend two on a header and still fit the tiers.
  - **Stats reads, it does not compute.** Every number on the screen is one the hero already carries (`attacks`, `saves`, `def`, `init`, `slots`, `critFrom`). If a formula changes, it changes in `attributes.json` and the screen follows; a number that appears here and nowhere in the hero is a bug in the sheet, not in the screen.
  - **A skill tile says where it stands without colour.** The outline gives learned, available and locked an outline colour each; the tiles also carry rank pips, and a locked one says what it is missing on the LEARN button when it is picked (`00`, "Color is never the only signal").
  - **The Skill Tree repaints in place.** Learning a rank spends a point that the top bar's chip, the Path chips and the tier headers all show, so one `paint()` redraws the whole screen from the hero rather than five listeners patching their own corner.
  - **A hero starts with 2 SP, not 1.** `01` section 6 promises **21 SP by level 20** and writes its four example builds against that total, but nineteen level-ups after a 1-point start give 20. The totals win, because the example builds are the part that has to be buyable: `attributes.json` now says `skillPointsAtLevel1: 2` and `skillPointsAtCap: 21`, and a data check confirms that what is handed out over twenty levels equals what is promised. Recorded under Open questions in case the intent was 20.

- **2026-09-19 — Gaining a level, and the bug it uncovered.** `src/systems/levelling.js` is `01` section 5: the curve, the 1d6 + VIG hit points, the Focus that re-derives itself, the skill point, and the attribute point at 4, 8, 12, 16 and 20. Five rulings:
  - **The level-up roll comes from the `loot` stream.** `05` section 11 names five streams and none of them is levelling, but `06` section 15 awards XP and loot in the same breath at the end of a fight: both are that fight's reward. It is a carried stream, so a reload cannot re-roll a level — which is the point of section 11.
  - **Levelling happens the moment the XP lands.** `06` section 15 step 5 says so, and `05` section 11 wants the outcome committed before it is shown, so the *fight* awards the XP and levels the hero as it ends. The Victory & Loot screen will read what already happened rather than cause it; a single award can be worth several levels, and each rolls its own hit points.
  - **Hit points are carried and everything else is re-derived.** A level rolls for HP and keeps the total; Focus, the Base Attack, the saves, DEF and the crit range are all recomputed from the attributes and the new level, and then the skills are folded back on top. Spending an attribute point later never re-rolls the hit points already gained.
  - **A raised maximum is handed over by whoever raised it.** `applySkillSheet` now only recomputes the maxima and clamps; learning a rank, being granted a free skill and gaining a level each hand the difference to the hero. Before, the sheet itself healed — so every rebuild would have healed the hero a little.
  - **The base sheet is the hero without any skill.** A level adds its hit points to *that*, not to the total a skill has already raised, or the skill's bonus would be counted again on the next rebuild.

  **The bug the tests found.** `applySkillSheet` built its working sheet with a shallow spread of the base, so `attacks` and `saves` were the base's own objects — and every rebuild added the skill's bonus to the very numbers it was measuring from. A hero with Weapon Training gained a silent, permanent +1 melee on each level-up, and their saves crept the same way. The fix is one line; the test that catches it rebuilds the sheet five times and checks the nested parts, which is where a shallow copy always shows.

- **2026-09-19 — Learning a skill.** `src/systems/skill-tree.js` is `01` section 6's gates: a tier opens at 0, 3, 6 and 10 points spent in its own Path, a capstone also wants an attribute of 15, a Crossroads skill wants 4 in each of its two Paths, and a rank costs 1. Five rulings:
  - **Only paid ranks count.** A hero's skills are `[{ id, rank, free }]`, where `free` is how many ranks an origin gave, and every count of "points spent in the Path" subtracts them. That is exactly what `01` section 3 promises — "it doesn't count against Path requirements" — and it means a Sellsword's free Weapon Training cannot be used to open Blade's tier II. A hero may still *pay* past a free rank, and the free one stays free.
  - **The free skill is granted at creation.** `finish` now hands it over, so a hero walks out of Create: Origin already knowing it and already getting its effect: a Sellsword's melee is +1 from the first step. It was being carried as a promise and given to nobody.
  - **A structural reason beats the purse.** A tier that is not open says `tierLocked` whether or not there is a point to spend, because that is the fact a player plans around; `noPoints` only ever appears on a skill they could otherwise take.
  - **A Crossroads skill has no tier.** It answers to its two Paths' point counts and nothing else, so it can be taken with nothing above tier I in either — which is what makes the hybrids reachable early, as the example builds assume.
  - **Forgetting keeps the gift.** A respec refunds every paid point and leaves the origin's free skill where it is, for 100 gp a level (`01` section 5).

  The document's four example builds at level 20 are the test: three of them are built point by point, in order, and have to fit inside 21 points and every gate. They do — with one exception, below.

- **2026-09-19 — `skills.json`, and what a skill's effect is made of.** `01` section 6's whole tree: four Paths of four tiers, six Crossroads hybrids, **51 skills and 68 skill points** — which is the document's own "about 68 available", and a good check on the transcription, since the ranks have to add up to it. Six rulings:
  - **An effect is one of four shapes.** A **sheet** effect is a number on the hero's own sheet, folded in when the skill is learned (+5 max HP a rank, +1 DEF a rank, a wider crit range). A **hook** effect names an event from `06` section 16 and the handler that runs on it. An **explore** effect is a bonus the dungeon systems read (`03`, `05`). An **action** block is what an active skill does when it is used. `npm run data` fails on an effect that is none of them, on a hook that names an event the engine does not fire, and on a handler nothing implements.
  - **A passive that is a number never touches the combat loop.** Toughness does not register anything: it raises `maxHp`, and the engine already reads `maxHp`. That is what keeps CLAUDE.md's "no special-case code paths" true — the loop never asks whether the hero has a skill.
  - **The sheet is rebuilt from a base, not accumulated.** `applySkillSheet` keeps the hero's pre-skill numbers and re-derives from them, so learning a rank twice, loading a save, or respeccing can never compound a bonus. A raised maximum hands the difference over at once, which is what learning Toughness mid-run should feel like.
  - **A conditional bonus waits for the system that can judge it.** Duelist's "+1 DEF while not using a shield" and Armor Training's "DR 1 in heavy armor" are on the sheet under their own names but add nothing yet, because the pack (`04`, Phase 5) is what says what is worn.
  - **A capstone must name its attribute.** The check fails on a tier IV skill without one, because `01` section 6 gates all four on a score of 15.
  - **What is live today is countable, and counted.** 21 passives apply in full; 19 active skills are waiting on a resolver for the Combat: Skills sheet; 11 more passives wait on a handler, each listed in `PENDING` with the phase it needs. `isLive` is deliberately strict — an active skill is *not* live merely because its action block exists.

  The four origins' free skills now check against this file: `npm run data` fails if an origin grants a skill the tree does not have, which it proved by catching a planted typo.

- **2026-09-19 — The derived statistics, actually reaching the game.** `src/systems/derived.js` landed with character creation, and its numbers were right; three of them were not being *read* by anything. Ticking the task off on the module alone was premature. What was missing:
  - **A hero's Vigor did nothing against poison.** `06` section 4 step 13 rolls the end-of-turn saves through the condition engine, which takes a `saveBonus` service — and `registerRules` was passing `undefined`, so it fell back to `() => 0`. Every hero rolled flat against Poisoned, Burning, Feared and Paralyzed however high their attributes were. The default is now the same reader the on-hit riders already used, so both paths agree and a Body of +3 turns a failing 9 into a passing 12.
  - **The flee roll had no attributes in it.** `06` section 14 is d20 + AGI mod + LCK mod; the fight was passing `hero.fleeBonus`, which nothing ever set.   - **Legality is a question with an answer, not a filter.** `legalityOf` returns `{ legal: false, why: 'webbed' }` and the screen dims the button with that reason, which is the UI rule about disabled buttons. Actions are matched by **tags**, so Feared's one `melee` entry stops melee attacks and melee skills alike, and `npm run data` fails if a reason has no line in `strings.json`. Rows are the whole of targeting for now: melee reaches the front row while anyone stands in it, and the back row only with Reach or once the front is empty (`02`); the −2 half cover that a bow pays is section 6's, and arrives with it.
  - **A telegraph counts the hero's turns, not rounds.** It remembers how many turns the hero had taken when it wound up and resolves on the monster's first turn after that number rises, which is exactly what section 5 asks for and is why initiative being rerolled every round cannot be exploited. Anything that stuns, sleeps, paralyses, petrifies or kills the monster cancels it, swept after every action.

  The death check at step 7 fires `zeroHP` and believes the answer, so Undying, Ferocity and Relentless are hooks that the Zero HP task registers; today nothing is registered and a unit at 0 HP dies. Attack resolution (section 6) is next, and it is what `resolveAction` will be.

- **2026-09-18 — The round, and the three initiative bands.** `src/engine/field.js` is `06` section 2, `src/engine/initiative.js` is section 3's Initiative, and `src/engine/round.js` is its ten numbered steps. `src/data/combat.json` holds the tables. Six rulings:

  - **A band beats any roll, and a band is a flag.** `actsFirst` (Quicksilver) and `actsLast` reach the same place from a potion, from the Slowed condition, or from the Zombie's own "always acts last", so nothing in the engine asks what kind of thing a unit is.   - **A fifth front-row monster stands in the back row.** Five Giant Rats are inside the 5-enemy crowd cap but one over a row's four. Setup overflows into the other row, where `02` says a melee monster waits and steps forward when there is room; a *summon* never overflows, because its ability names its row and "a summon that doesn't fit simply fails" (`06` section 13). A monster over the crowd cap is not on the field at all, and is listed in `combat.turnedAway`.
  - **Objects stand but do not act.** A valve or the Phylactery takes a back-row spot, counts toward neither the crowd cap nor morale nor the hero's flee roll, and gets no turn unless it carries `acts: true` — which is how the Hydra's body will take one.

The turn itself is the caller's `takeTurn`, so the round's order is proved — 49 tests — before a single attack exists. `combatOver` implements section 15's three endings now, including the unburned Fallen troll that keeps a fight alive, so the loop has something honest to ask.

- **2026-09-18 — The event hook system.** `src/engine/hooks.js` is `06` section 16: one register per fight, fourteen events fired in the section's order, and a payload the hooks read and change in place. Four rulings:
  - **The documented order is a name, not a number.** A hook registered as `poisoned`, `crit` or `cleave` sorts itself by where `06` section 16's own table lists it; a hook whose name the table does not list runs after the listed ones, in registration order. `ORDER` in the module is that table, transcribed. A hook may still give an explicit `order` when it needs one.
  - **Hook priority is decided at firing time**, not at registration: a hook belongs to a unit, and whether it runs in the target's group, the attacker's, or the field's depends on who the event is about. One `hit` hook on a monster is therefore first when it is hit and second when it hits.
  - **Cancelling stops the chain.** `payload.cancel()` — or a hook returning `false` — ends that event, which is how Counterspell stops an action and Blink turns a hit into a miss. Nothing after it runs.
  - **Uses are the framework's job**, not each skill's: `once: true` for Undying, and `limit: { uses, per: 'turn' | 'round' | 'combat' | 'rest' }` for Cleave's once per turn and Ferocity's once per combat, reset by the engine at the matching boundary (`06` section 3 step 2 resets the reaction flags this way).

- **2026-09-18 — Conditions.** `src/data/conditions.json` is `01` section 7's table as data and `src/engine/conditions.js` is the engine over it: durations, stacking, control immunity and Grit. Six rulings:

  Its UMD wrapper picks between `module.exports` and a global. `package.json` sets `"type": "module"`, so the file is an ES module everywhere it is used — Node, esbuild and Vite alike — and **both** branches fail there: top-level `this` is `undefined`, `self` does not exist in Node, and under Vite `module` is a read-only namespace object whose `default` cannot be assigned. Every load threw before the factory ran.

  The wrapper is reduced to calling the factory, and ES module exports are appended. **The factory itself is untouched.** Verified byte-identical against the pristine file from git: 180 generations (3 grid sizes x 60 seeds) plus all four exported helpers and the TILE enum produce identical output for the same sequence of `Math.random` draws.

  The alternatives were worse: a `src/dungeon/package.json` marking the directory CommonJS would force every new file there to be `.mjs`, against the filenames `07` itself specifies; and loading the file through a global side effect broke under bundling, where evaluation order is not guaranteed.

  **`src/dungeon/raycaster.js` has the same wrapper and will need the same change** when `view.js` is built. It is left alone until then, so the change can be verified against something that renders.

- **2026-09-18 — floors.json scope and two superseded numbers.** `floors.json` holds the floor table from `05` section 2 plus the per-floor counts from section 3 (steps 6-9), because the next Phase 2 tasks consume exactly those and CLAUDE.md forbids magic numbers in logic. Restocking (`05` section 8) is left out; Phase 6 owns it. Two numbers are kept for the record but cannot currently be honoured, because the vendored generator replaced `05` section 3 steps 1-4:
  - **Floor 3's twistier corridors** (80% newest cell in the growing-tree carve). The vendored generator takes only `roomDensity`, with no maze-bias knob, so floor 3 is stored as `corridorStyle: "twisty"` and gets its identity from goblin camps instead.
  - **The 70% room-to-corridor door rate and the 8% extra-connector rate.** The generator places its own doors and loops.

  **Room counts reach the generator as a density.** The documents give room counts; the module takes `roomDensity` and derives `maxRooms = floor(w * h * 0.08 * density)`. `roomDensityFor()` inverts that from the top of each floor's range, adding half a room first so the generator's own `floor()` cannot land a room short. It is a cap on attempts, not a promise, so the builder still has to check the range.

- **2026-09-18 — Seeded RNG details.** Two choices in `src/engine/rng.js` that affect what replays:
  - **A cancelled advantage still draws two dice.** `06` section 6 step 5 says one advantage and one disadvantage cancel. The stream draws both dice either way and keeps the first, so a roll costs the same two draws however the caller reached the cancellation, and a replay of the same fight stays in step.
  - **Regenerating a floor uses an attempt number, not literally `seed + 1`.** `05` section 3 says a floor failing the solvability check is regenerated with `seed + 1`. `layoutStream(masterSeed, floor, attempt)` mixes the attempt into the seed instead, which is deterministic in the same way and cannot collide with a neighbouring floor's stream.

- **2026-09-18 — Phase 1 shell choices.** Six small rulings the documents did not settle, each behind a named constant:
  - **Text size setting.** `--text-scale` multiplies body and hint text only; Bungee labels and titles are sized to their boxes and do not scale. S is 1.00 and gives the documented minimums on a 390 px frame (hint 12 px, body 14 px, button 18 px); M is 1.08 (the mockups) and L is 1.20. In `src/styles/tokens.css`.
  - **The 2-row tap target rule** applies to regions placed on the screen grid. Controls inside a `.scroll` panel follow the mockups instead, which use 52 px rows; a 14-row settings list cannot hold nine labelled 2-row controls. Checked by `npm run check`.
  - **EXPORT / IMPORT in the wide Settings screen** take rows 8–9, not the outline's row 9 alone, so they keep the 2-row minimum.
  - **The release is `dist/index.html` plus four files.** JS, CSS, and fonts are inlined into the one page as the outline says; `sw.js`, `manifest.webmanifest`, and the icons stay separate because an installable app needs real URLs for them.
  - **Screens a later phase will add** disable their button with the reason "Not built yet" rather than going missing, so the layout never shifts as phases land. `router.has(id)` reports what exists.
  - **The URL fragment may name a screen** (`#settings`), which is how `npm run check` and `npm run shots` open each one. Unknown fragments start at the Title screen.

## Open questions

- **What a hero with no weapon hits with.** `04` never expects one, because it never lets go of the kit. `items.json` carries `rules.unarmed` — **1d2 crush**, the smallest thing that is not nothing — and `attackWith` falls back to it. (Default: keep it; if bare hands should be a real option, `04` section 2 needs a row.)

- **A very low Might cannot carry its own kit.** Slots are 10 + (MIG mod x 2), so a Might of 3 leaves 4 slots, and the Cutpurse's kit needs 6. The overflow is handed back and listed on `hero.kitLeftBehind`. (Default: leave it — the slot rule working as written — unless creation should guarantee the kit fits.)

- **What a found-only item sells for.** `04` prices most things, but the Healing Herb, the Skeleton Key, Dragonscale Mail and every unique and legendary have no number, and section 1's "shops buy at 50%" has nothing to halve. Troll Blood is the one the document settles (100 gp). Everything else sells for 0 until the Shop is built. (Default: half of a listed cost, nothing where there is none; if uniques should be sellable, they need prices in `04` section 13.)

- **Where the four harmful potions come from.** Section 8 says "found only" and section 14's d12 tables never roll one, so nothing in the game can produce one yet. They are marked `harmful` and kept out of every loot pool. (Default: chest contents in Phase 7 place them, at the same rate as a utility potion.)

- **An Ironman hero reaches level 5 about a third of the time.** `npm run climb -- 24 --ironman` measures it: 8 of 24, with 16 deaths over 449 fights — roughly one fight in twenty-eight kills a hero who is playing carefully. Floors 1 and 2 are meant to be survivable, and Phase 5's potions and Phase 6's temple will both push this up, so the number is recorded rather than acted on. Phase 7's balance simulator is where it gets settled. (Default: leave it; re-measure once potions exist.)

- **Does a hero start with 1 skill point or 2?** `01` section 6 says both: line 110 gives "1 SP at level 1" on top of 1 per level, and lines 137 and 221 promise "21 SP by level 20" and write the example builds at 21. Nineteen level-ups make those differ by one. The totals win for now (start with 2, reach 21), because the example builds have to be buyable. If the intent was 20, the fix is `skillPointsAtLevel1: 1` in `attributes.json` and one point out of each example build.

- **The Pure Warrior example build cannot buy its own Spirit 4.** `01` section 6's example reads "Spirit 4 (Resilience ×2, Mend, Cleanse)", but Resilience and Cleanse are Spirit tier II, which section 6 opens at **3 points already spent in that Path**, and Mend is the only tier I skill on that list. The first Resilience would be bought with 1 point in the Path. The tier gate is a rule and the example is a sketch, so the gate wins and `test/skill-tree.test.js` spends those four points on a legal Spirit 4 (Mend, Keen Senses ×2, Resilience) instead. If the example is meant to be exactly buyable, the fix is in the example rather than in the gate — or Resilience moves to tier I.

- **Which RNG stream a hazard rolls on.** Settled for the two kinds that exist: wandering checks and noise draw from `encounter` (`05` section 11), and an action roll like a door bash draws from `combat`, with the other d20s. What is still open is the rest of the step-time rolls: a spinner's new facing, a fountain's d6, a pole breaking. (Default: follow the same split — a d20 the hero rolls goes to `combat`, anything that decides what the dungeon does goes to `encounter`.)
- **Floors 1–3 cannot reach the critical-path pacing target.** `05` section 2 asks for 150–300 steps from arrival to the boss, and defines the critical path as the *shortest* walking route (section 3 step 5). The full sweep (10,000 seeds × 10 floors) measures what each grid size can actually produce:

  | Floors | Grid | Median steps | Within 150–300 |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 1–3 | 33 × 33 | 104–107 | 7–9% |
  | 4–7 | 41 × 41 | 158–162 | 58–61% |
  | 8–10 | 49 × 49 | 209–222 | 83% |

  This is the grid, not the placement: the arrival point chosen averages 116 steps against a theoretical ceiling of 121 — within 4% of the farthest tile that exists on a 33 × 33 map. Three ways out: accept that early floors are shorter (a quick floor 1 reads as intentional), enlarge floors 1–3, or read the target as including exploration rather than the beeline. Until it is settled, `test/sweep.test.js` locks in the measured medians so pacing cannot quietly get worse, and states the shortfall as a fact rather than a wish. (Default: accept shorter early floors.)
- **Tap targets inside scrolling lists.** The build outline's own Settings mockup uses 52 px segmented controls, which is under the 2-row minimum (about 80 px). Phase 1 reads the minimum as applying to grid-placed regions only. Confirm, or raise the in-list controls and accept more scrolling.
- **Letterbox.** Space left over in either frame is plain black bars. Keep that, or fill the bars with a subtle dungeon pattern? (Default: plain black.)
- **Tablet in portrait.** Currently the phone layout scaled up, capped at 72 px units. Worth a wider two-pane portrait layout later, or leave it? (Default: leave it.)
- **Art style.** The raycaster starts with its flat shaded fallback (no textures). Wall textures are plain ImageData when someone makes them. Monster and portrait art are still placeholders. Pixel art, line art, or text-only cards? (Default: bold line-art walls; enemy cards are text with HP bars.)
- **Sound.** No sound design is specified yet. (Default: short synthesized effects via Web Audio; no music until decided.)
- **Story.** The reason for descending, town characters, and the ending text aren't written yet. (Default: a short intro card and an ending card with placeholder text.)
- **Onboarding text.** The floor 1 tips need wording. (Default: one short sentence per first-time event.)
- **Game title.** "Underkeep" is a working title.
